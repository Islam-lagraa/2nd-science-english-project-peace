Peace Project

Everyone deserves to live in peace

This project looks at three wars where ordinary people, especially children, are suffering. We want to understand what is happening and how we can help.

Three wars, one message

War brings the same pain everywhere: lost homes, hunger, fear, and the death of innocent people. Killing civilians is wrong, no matter who does it. Below are three crises where people need our attention.

Ukraine and Russia

In 2022, Russia invaded Ukraine. The UN has confirmed more than 16,000 civilian deaths since then, including 803 children. Ukrainian families need safety and peace.

Learn more

Palestine and Israel

For many years, Palestinians have lived under Israeli occupation, and Gaza has been blocked in since 2007. Israel has attacked Gaza many times, and since 2023 its war has destroyed homes, hospitals and families. The UN says most people in Gaza still don’t have enough food, shelter or clean water.

Learn more

Sudan

In 2023, a war started in Sudan between the army and a group called the RSF. About 12 million people have had to leave their homes. It is one of the worst crises in the world, but almost nobody talks about it.

Learn more

The UN must do more

The United Nations was made to protect peace, but it often cannot act. Five powerful countries on its Security Council can use a veto to block any decision. In 2025, two US vetoes blocked action on Gaza and two Russian vetoes blocked action on Ukraine. The Council also passed only 44 resolutions that year, the fewest since 1991. A veto also blocked a call for a ceasefire in Sudan. Aid is short too: the UN asked for $45 billion in 2025 but got only $12 billion. Many UN workers do brave work, but they need support. We believe the UN must treat all civilians equally, limit the veto when civilians are being killed, and protect aid workers.

How can we help

Everyone can do something. First, learn the facts from trusted sources like the UN, the Red Cross and Red Crescent, and respected news sites. Don’t share fake news. Second, speak up. Tell your family, friends and classmates what you learned, and ask leaders to support peace and protect civilians. Third, give money if you can. Groups like UNICEF, the World Food Programme, the Red Cross and Red Crescent, and Doctors Without Borders help in all three crises. Even a small school fundraiser can make a difference. Finally, be kind. Don’t hate ordinary people because of their country or religion. Peace starts with respect.
